第一次世界大战（World War I）

> 1914–1918 · 轴1 编年叙事

中文：第一次战争让密码学第一次成为生死攸关的工业。德军用 ADFGVX（结合换位与替换的格子密码）加密无线电，英军 40 号房逐份破译；美军的萨福德接手美国版「40 号房」，破译德军潜艇密码。这一代有标志性的胜利：1917 年英军破译齐默尔曼电报（德国外长密令墨西哥进攻美国），直接推动美国参战——这是「一份密电改变战争走向」的经典。Playfair 双表密码被英军广泛使用，恩尼格玛的前身（Scherbius 的转子机）开始出现。第一次大战证明：电报加密的胜负，直接决定战线。机器还没登场，但舞台已经搭好。

English: The first war made cryptography a life-or-death industry for the first time. Germany used ADFGVX (a grid cipher combining transposition and substitution) for its radio traffic, and Britain’s Room 40 broke it piece by piece; the American Safford took over the US "Room 40" and broke German submarine codes. This generation has a landmark victory: in 1917 the British cracked the Zimmermann Telegram (the German Foreign Secretary secretly urging Mexico to attack America), directly pushing the US into the war — the classic case of "one telegram changes the course of the war." Playfair’s double-square cipher was widely used by the British army, and the precursor of Enigma (Scherbius’s rotor machine) appeared. WWI proved that the outcome of telegraph encryption directly decides the front lines. Machines had not yet arrived, but the stage was set.
